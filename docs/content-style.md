# Content and Copy Standards

> Owns voice, approved lines and their placement, banned words, claim safety, and naming rules.
> Wins on any wording question. Visual questions belong to `design-system.md`.
>
> Every rule here is traceable to `brief.md` §n, or marked **inference** / **PROVISIONAL**.

---

## 1. The voice, as a testable standard

§19 lists nine adjectives — premium, calm, precise, elegant, confident, intelligent, sensory,
minimal, considered. Adjectives are not actionable, so the operative standard is §19's worked
contrast, which is the single most useful artefact in the brief:

> **Write this:** Golden tips. Deep character. Assam at its most distinguished.
>
> **Not this:** Experience the ultimate luxurious richness of our exceptionally premium and
> highly beneficial tea sourced from the finest regions of Assam.

**The mechanics that produce the first sentence** (*inference*, extracted from the contrast):

| Rule | Bad | Good |
|---|---|---|
| Short declaratives; fragments are allowed | "Our tea is one that has been carefully selected from…" | "Selected for character." |
| Concrete nouns over abstract ones | "an experience of luxury" | "golden tips", "a whisked bowl", "the second infusion" |
| Few adjectives, and never stacked | "exceptionally premium, highly beneficial" | "distinguished" |
| **No intensifiers at all** | ultimate, exceptionally, highly, truly, incredibly, very | *(delete them)* |
| Confidence through omission | "we believe this is one of the finest…" | "A rare expression of Assam." |
| Never explain the premium | "worth every rupee because…" | *(let the product page do it)* |

**The test:** if removing a word does not change the meaning, it was decoration. Remove it.
This mirrors §60's design filter — *would removing one element improve it?*

### Approved reference sentences (§20)

Use these as calibration, not as copy to reuse verbatim:

- "From the upper belts of Assam, selected for its character."
- "Tea deserves time."
- "A rare expression of Assam."
- "Delicate by nature. Precise by craft."
- "Made for the moments you have earned."
- "Three expressions. One origin."

## 2. The brand lines and where each is allowed

§6 is the most protective rule in the verbal system: **these lines must not all appear together.**
Stacking them produces exactly the over-selling the brand rejects (§48).

| Line | Role | Belongs on | Must NOT appear |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Earned, Not Indulged.** | Emotional philosophy (§3, §5) | Homepage hero, brand film, campaign, packaging brand-line slot | Product pages, cart, checkout, transactional email |
| **Exceptional Tea. Uncompromising Quality.** | Product and quality promise (§62) | Quality/craft sections, "Our Story", packaging | Alongside the line above on the same screen |
| **Quality has no excuses.** | Internal belief (§62) | Internal; campaign use sparingly | Default site copy. It is a conviction, not a tagline |
| **Three expressions. One origin.** | Collection architecture (§13) | Collection page, range modules, gift sets | Individual product pages — it describes the set, not the item |
| **Pour slowly. Savour deeply. You've earned it.** | Ritual and lifestyle (§62) | Ritual pages, preparation content, unboxing insert, social | Commercial surfaces — cart, checkout, pricing |
| **Three teas. Three distinct journeys.** | Secondary collection line (§13) | Alternative to the platform line where variety is the point | Same screen as "Three expressions. One origin." |

**PROVISIONAL rule (*inference*):** at most **one** brand line per screen, and never two in the
same viewport. *Overturned by:* a client instruction that a specific pairing is approved.

## 3. Banned words

§46 covers claims. There is no tone equivalent in the brief — `risks.md` **R-27** records that
gap. The list below is **PROVISIONAL**, built from §19, §20 and §48.

**Never, on any surface:**

- *Indulgence framing* — treat yourself, indulge, guilty pleasure, spoil yourself, decadent.
  These are the literal antonym of "Earned, Not Indulged" and invert the brand rather than
  merely weakening it.
- *Intensifiers* — ultimate, exceptionally, incredibly, truly, super, highly, absolutely, very.
- *Mass-market enthusiasm* — amazing, delicious, must-have, yummy, irresistible, obsessed.
- *Urgency and scarcity mechanics* — hurry, last chance, don't miss out, selling fast, limited
  time only. §50 rules out the commercial version; §3 rules out the emotional version, because a
  reward earned over time cannot be an impulse.
- *Discount vocabulary* — sale, off, deal, bargain, save ₹, flash. See §49/§50.
- *Everyday-habit framing* — your daily cup, everyday luxury, morning essential. Dissolves the
  reward frame (*inference*).
- *Superlatives and absolutes* — world's best, India's number one, the finest, 100% pure (§20, §46).

**Do not use until resolved:** "Hukhmal", "halter" (§17 / R-08); "organic", "natural" (§46 / R-10);
any sub-regional origin term including "Deenukhiya" (§16 / R-03). This applies to alt text, meta
descriptions, URL slugs and structured data as much as to visible copy.

## 4. Claim safety

RejuveLuxe is a food and beverage brand, not a nutraceutical (§45). The operative distinction:
**composition is not a claim.** Naming a compound is safe; attributing an effect to it is not.

| Posture | Language | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Safe** | Sensory description; preparation guidance; leaf grade and processing *once verified*; origin *once validated*; naming naturally occurring compounds without attributing effects | §45, §47 |
| **Needs substantiation first** | Any nutrient or health claim; "antioxidant" framed as a benefit rather than a constituent; comparative quality claims; geographical-origin claims | §46 |
| **Requires certification** | "Organic" — valid certification under India's organic framework. Supplier assurance is not sufficient | §46 |
| **Never** | Disease prevention, cure or management; weight loss, detox, immunity, insulin, blood sugar, metabolism, inflammation, gut health, cardiovascular; absolutes (100% pure, world's best, India's number one) | §46, §20 |

**The operating rule:** name compounds without attributing effects, and let sensory and craft
language carry desirability. §47's own preferred list already points here. **The product must
remain desirable with zero health claims** — if it isn't, the problem is the product story, not
the missing claim.

**Hard gate (§60, first filter):** every factual claim must be verified before it ships. Origin,
process, certification, compound effects. This gate comes first because it is the only one with
legal consequences. See `risks.md` R-01, R-03, R-06, R-10.

## 5. The product story framework (§39)

Every product story — packaging, website, marketing — answers six questions, in this order:

| # | Question | Answers with | Current status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Where is it from? | Origin | **Blocked at "Assam"** until R-03 resolves |
| 2 | What is selected? | Leaf, bud or tip | Available — §12 gives this per hero |
| 3 | How is it made? | Craft | **Matcha blocked** by R-01; Silver Needle and Golden Tips have working process language |
| 4 | What does it taste like? | Sensory experience | **Blocked for all three** — no sensory vocabulary exists (R-29). Closed by cupping, not writing |
| 5 | How do I prepare it? | Ritual | Available for Matcha (§33); brewing parameters undefined for the others |
| 6 | Why does it matter? | Meaning | Available — territory (FOCUS / ELEGANCE / LEGACY) supplies it |

**Two of six are fully blocked and a third is partly blocked.** No product page is complete until
R-01, R-03 and R-29 resolve. A PDP shipped without taste notes fails §38, which mandates them.

## 7.1 Dashes: em and en dashes are banned

Numbered `7.1` rather than inserted as a new section because other documents cite this one by
section number; renumbering §8 and §9 would break those references.

**The rule, and it is absolute: no em dash (`—`) and no en dash (`–`) appears anywhere a customer
can see it.** Sayon, 20 August 2026: *"add a major rule that no em or en dashes are used anywhere
throughout the website. only hyphens if needed."*

### What "anywhere a customer can see it" covers

Visible copy, headings, product fields, button and link labels, page titles and meta descriptions,
`alt` text and `aria-label`s, form labels, validation and error messages, transactional email, and
anything rendered out of the catalogue or any future CMS field. §8 already rules that alt text and
metadata follow the same standard as visible copy; this rule inherits that scope.

### What to write instead

| Instead of | Use | Worked example |
|---|---|---|
| Em dash before an aside | A full stop, or recast | `not variations on one idea — they are a ladder` → `not variations on one idea. They are a ladder` |
| Em dash introducing an explanation | A colon | `a considered gift — the gift boxes` → `a considered gift: the gift boxes` |
| Em dash joining two clauses | A comma | `ritual and taste — and how to get the most` → `ritual and taste, and how to get the most` |
| En dash for a numeric range | A hyphen | `2–3 days` → `2-3 days` |
| Em dash as a separator between fields | A middle dot `·` | `Ships across India · Dispatched in 2-3 days` |

**The hyphen is the only dash character permitted**, and only where one is genuinely needed:
numeric ranges and compound modifiers. A hyphen is never a stand-in for a comma or a colon. If a
sentence seems to need an em dash, the sentence is doing two jobs and should be two sentences.

### Why this earns a rule rather than a preference

Recasting removes the dash and usually shortens the line, which is what §1's voice asks for and
what §9's filter question 3 tests. The em dash is also the most recognisable tell of machine-written
copy, and this site's copy must not read as generated.

One further reason, **from memory and unverified for this project**: the em dash sits outside the
GSM-7 alphabet used by SMS, so a single one can push a message into UCS-2 encoding and cut its
length from 160 characters to 70. That matters once TRAI DLT transactional SMS is live. Worth
confirming against whichever gateway is chosen rather than trusting this line.

## 8. Per-surface rules

| Surface | Rules |
|---|---|
| **Homepage hero** | One line only — "EARNED, NOT INDULGED." plus at most one supporting sentence (§37). No stacked lines, no CTA above the fold competing with the statement |
| **Product page** | The six-question framework (§5 above) in §38's field order. Taste notes are mandatory and currently blocked. No claims catalogue |
| **Packaging front** | §31's hierarchy: logo → product name → territory → origin → short descriptor → net quantity → statutory → brand line. **The front is never a claims catalogue** — this is simultaneously a design discipline and a legal safeguard |
| **Cart and checkout** | Functional and quiet. No brand lines, no ritual language, no persuasion. The buyer has already decided; anything else is noise |
| **Transactional email** | Plain, precise, informative. Confirmations are not campaigns |
| **Journal** | The place where longer form is permitted. Still no intensifiers |
| **Festive and events** | Festive relevance expressed **within** the existing system (§44). Not louder, not more colourful, not discount-led. "Maintain brand hygiene" |
| **Alt text and metadata** | Same rules as visible copy. Quarantined terms (§3 above) apply here too. This is where unverified claims usually leak |
| **Page title (browser tab, search result)** | **Every route has its own title naming that page.** One or two words, then ` · RejuveLuxe`. The suffix is applied automatically by the root layout's title template, so a page declares only its own part and the separator is never hand-typed. **Keep the page part short** — a browser tab shows roughly 24 characters before truncating, so "Shop" beats "Shop All Teas and the Ritual Set". Product pages use the product name. The homepage sets the whole string itself, because a title template does not apply to its own route segment |

## 9. The copy filter

Before any copy ships, in this order (§60, ordered by consequence):

1. **Is every factual claim verified?** If not, it does not ship. Legal consequences.
2. **Is it specific to this product**, or could it describe any premium tea?
3. **Would removing one element improve it?** If yes, remove it.
4. **Does it address one person**, with no implied spectator? (§23)
5. **Does it strengthen "Earned, Not Indulged"** — or quietly invert it into indulgence?
6. **Would the customer understand why this costs what it costs**, without being told?

Any "no" sends the copy back.

**Before all six, one mechanical check that needs no judgement:** grep the copy for `—` and `–`.
§7.1 bans both outright, and unlike the questions above this one has a single right answer, so it
costs nothing to run first. The same grep belongs in whatever check command the README eventually
documents.
